BATTERY STORAGE SITING REPORT · GREAT BRITAIN

Know whether a battery site is worth pursuing, and what would end it.

The battery storage siting report is an Arithim report on one site in Great Britain. It says whether the site is worth pursuing and why, covering grid, planning constraints, land and economics, with the source and date of every figure.

WHAT YOU GET

What does the report contain?

One report answers the questions a developer asks before spending money on a site.

A verdict with reasons

Whether the site is worth pursuing, and what would end it if it fails.

Constraints on a map

Designations, flood zones, heritage, green belt, peat and farmland grade.

Grid headroom

The nearest substations, their published headroom and its date, Gate 2 context and the registered projects already holding capacity at the node. It shows whether known import headroom covers your battery, with reinforcement cost and delay as labelled assumptions.

Storage saturation

Operating, consented and pending battery schemes within 2 km and 5 km, with pending applications within 2 km named.

Planning route

The consent route for the nation and the planning authority's record.

Economics

NPV in low, central and high cases, IRR, break-even capex per kWh, and the result with and without the embedded export credit.

WHAT YOU SEND

What do I send?

  • A location: a point, a drawn boundary, or a KML, KMZ, GeoJSON or zipped shapefile
  • The battery size in MW and its duration in hours (1, 2 or 4 hours are modelled)
  • Optionally, a solar site to pair it with

You receive a PDF, with a Word copy and an Excel model on request. An appendix lists the source and licence of every dataset. The data is open public data from NESO, the network operators, the national nature and heritage agencies, the flood agencies and the planning registers.

WHO IT IS FOR

Who is the battery storage siting report for?

Battery developers, their land teams and the landowners they approach. A developer asks whether to spend money on the site and what ends it if it fails. A landowner asks whether the site is any good for a battery, and whether an offer is reasonable for the location.

LIMITS

What does the report not do?

We state these on the page because they change what you can rely on.

  • Great Britain only. Northern Ireland is not covered.
  • Headroom is the network operator's published figure on its stated date. It is not a connection offer, and contracted queue positions are only partly public.
  • The engine works on 50 m pixels in cells of about 0.7 km², so small buffers and narrow features are indicative.
  • Aerodrome and Ministry of Defence safeguarding zones are not covered.
  • Land ownership is limited to registered title extents in England and Wales, with no owner names, and nothing for Scotland.
  • Revenue is modelled from public figures and labelled assumptions. It is not a merchant revenue forecast.
  • It does not replace the site studies a planning application needs: ecology, landscape and visual, glint and glare, flood risk, heritage and archaeology, ground conditions and noise.
FREQUENTLY ASKED

Questions about Battery storage siting report

Where does the grid information come from, and how current is it?

From NESO's registers and each network operator's published headroom and capacity registers. Every figure in the report carries its source and the date that source published it. The registers are archived weekly, so changes such as new queue entries show up.

Does it cover Scotland and Wales?

Yes, for grid, designations, heritage, flood, farmland and peat. Land title extents cover England and Wales only.

Can I change the assumptions?

Yes. Battery size, duration, capex, reinforcement treatment and the export credit can be changed, and the report shows what moved as a result. Defaults are labelled as assumptions with their basis.

Does the report predict a connection offer or a planning decision?

No. It shows published headroom and the planning record, and it labels cost and delay as assumptions. The network operator and the planning authority decide.

How long does it take, and how do I order?

Three working days. You can order by email today. Ordering through an API is in early access for named customers.
