Speaker notes
  1. 01

    Operational intelligence vs automation

    Both matter, and they solve different problems. Confusing the two is how AI projects end up impressive in a demo and useless in production.

  2. 02

    Each one solves a different problem.

    Automation changes how one task gets done. Operational intelligence changes how well people see, decide and act across many tasks.

  3. 03

    Same input, same rule, same result.

    Automation is dependable because the step does not change. When the right answer depends on context, a fixed rule will eventually get it wrong.

  4. 04

    When the right action depends on what else is true.

    Here the value comes from bringing the right information to the person deciding, at the moment they decide.

  5. 05

    “Automation for dependable execution. Intelligence for coordination and control.”

    Most operations need both. The question is which one a given problem needs.

  6. 06

    Strong systems usually contain both.

    Before buying either, describe the problem. If the step is fixed, automate it. If the right action depends on changing context, start with intelligence.

Automation moves a task from manual execution to structured execution. Operational intelligence improves how information, judgement, and action move through a business.

Both matter. But they solve different problems, and confusing the two is how AI projects end up impressive in a demo and useless in production.

Automation is useful when a repeatable step can be handled consistently: the same input, the same rule, the same result, every time. Operational intelligence is useful when teams need visibility, context, evidence, or decision support across a changing environment, where the "right" action depends on what else is true at that moment.

Automation for dependable execution. Intelligence for coordination and control.

Strong systems usually contain both. The discipline is knowing which one a given problem actually needs, and not paying for the wrong one.
