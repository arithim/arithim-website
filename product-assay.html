THE PROBLEM

Why would a company need a list of its AI models?

A customer, a supplier or a supervisor may ask which models you depend on, and a list kept by hand misses models in two places.

Calls to a provider's API

An internal application sends requests to a hosted model. The destination shows up in network logs, code, configuration and cloud billing, but the host name does not always tell you which model answered. A call to a cloud provider can be served by an open-weight model of any origin.

Models on your own GPUs

A team runs an open-weight model on an in-house cluster. Nothing leaves the building, so network logs show nothing. Assay looks at the inference servers, the container images and the model files themselves.

In October 2026 the US Homeland Security Department told civilian federal agencies to remove DeepSeek products and third-party tools built on them (TechCrunch, 20 October 2026). That notice covers US federal civilian agencies. It is not UK law, and as of October 2026 we know of no UK rule that bans a specific model.

HOW IT WORKS

How does Assay find the models, and how often?

It reads data you already produce and matches it against a registry of AI endpoints, model families, file hashes and lineage. A hint from one source is a lead. A second source that agrees makes it a finding, and where only one source supports a result, the report says so.

Assay throttles itself, runs off-peak, and never touches GPU compute. You hold a switch that stops it. When a model is newly ruled out, past data is re-matched at once with no new load on your systems.

WHAT WE NEED

What does my team need to open up?

All access is read-only. We ask for the least we need and agree each item with you before we start.

Network logs

A read-only export of DNS, firewall or proxy logs, or a forwarder pointing at our collector.

Cloud accounts

A read-only role in each account, covering audit logs, model deployments and billing.

Code repositories

Read-only access through an app that you approve and can revoke.

GPU hosts

A read-only login, or a script you run yourselves and send back.

A home for the collector

One virtual machine or container with outbound HTTPS only. Nothing inbound.

A contact and a scope letter

A named technical contact, and a signed note of what we may scan, when, and from where.

We do not need your prompts or payloads, administrator rights, an inline proxy, or agents on laptops for a first scan. Logs can hold personal data such as IP addresses and usernames, so we agree data processing terms with you first and keep what we collect to metadata.

LIMITS

What can Assay not see?

Each report lists these as stated limits, with the signal that partly covers each one.

  • Devices off your network. A laptop on home broadband, or a tunnel to a relay, can hide a call. Endpoint tools you already run and a signed statement from your team cover part of this.
  • Models inside supplier products. If a supplier runs a model on its own servers, your network never sees it. Questionnaires and contract terms are the control here.
  • Hidden server names. Newer TLS features can encrypt the server name, which weakens DNS signals. Code, cloud and billing records fill the gap.
  • Air-gapped sites. We cannot reach them over a network. We supply an offline scanner and a signed bundle that you return to us.
AFTER THE FIRST REPORT

What happens after the first report?

What you do with the findings is your decision. These are the next steps we can help with.

Replace a model

Swap a flagged model for an approved one, rewrite the integration and test that quality holds.

Rescan on a schedule

A report goes stale. Quarterly rescans keep the attestation current.

Monitor between scans

An alert when a new application starts calling an endpoint you have ruled out.

Route calls through a gateway

A single path that blocks ruled-out endpoints and logs every call. It sits in the traffic path, so it is optional and comes later.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

Questions about Assay

What is Assay?

Assay is an Arithim service that lists the AI models a company uses and shows where each one runs. It covers models your applications call over an API and open-weight models on your own GPUs. It flags the ones you have ruled out and produces a dated report with its scope, method and confidence stated.

Do you read our prompts or the data sent to models?

No. We collect metadata: destination names, timing, volume and the application making the call. We do not store prompts or responses.

Will Assay slow our systems down?

It should not. It reads logs and cloud records you already have, in read-only mode, at low rates and outside peak hours. On GPU hosts it checks file sizes and dates first and never uses GPU compute. You hold a switch that stops it.

Is the Assay report a certificate?

No. It is an attestation report that states its scope, date and method. It says what we found and did not find in that scope, on that date. It does not claim to prove that no banned model exists anywhere in your company, so we do not call it a certificate.

Which models does Assay flag?

The models on the list you set. We start from the models named in the rules you are subject to, and our registry records which model family sits behind an endpoint or a model file. Every report names the registry version it used.

Can Assay tell which model is behind an endpoint?

Not from the host name alone. A call to a US cloud provider can be served by an open-weight model of any origin. We compare several sources, give each finding a confidence level, and mark anything we could not confirm as open.

Do we need to install anything?

One collector, as a virtual machine or container inside your network, with outbound HTTPS only. A first scan needs no agents on laptops.

Does a UK firm have to do this?

As of October 2026 we know of no UK rule that bans a specific AI model. The US notice covers federal civilian agencies. UK firms usually come to us because a customer, a supplier or their own risk team has asked which models they use. We will tell you if you do not need Assay.

How much does Assay cost?

We agree the scope in a call, then quote. The price depends on how many networks, cloud accounts, repositories and GPU hosts are in scope.

Why is it called Assay?

An assay is a test of what something is made of. We test which models an estate contains and say what we found, in what scope and on what date. UK assay offices test and mark precious metals. We are not an assay office, and our report is not an official mark.

Want to know what Assay would scan in your estate?

Tell us roughly what you run: your networks, cloud accounts and any GPU clusters. We will tell you what we would scan, what we could not see, and whether it is worth doing.
