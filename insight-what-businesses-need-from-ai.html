Speaker notes
  1. 01

    What most businesses actually need from AI

    Most organisations do not need a louder AI strategy. They need a clearer operating model for where AI can reduce friction and support better decisions.

  2. 02

    Start with ordinary questions about the work.

    Useful AI work rarely starts with a model. It starts with the places where the business already loses time.

  3. 03

    Where does the work get stuck?

    Each answer points at a place where AI might reduce friction. Ask them of one team before you ask them of the whole organisation.

  4. 04

    Smaller and more disciplined than the hype.

    The implementation path is often smaller than the hype suggests. Automation or intelligence comes last, once the workflow and the data are clear.

  5. 05

    “AI becomes valuable when it is part of a system people can trust, operate, and improve.”

    The model is one part. The people who run it, the data it reads and the way it gets fixed decide whether it lasts.

  6. 06

    Will it survive a normal Tuesday?

    A demo shows what a model can do once. A system people can run, trust and improve is what keeps working on an ordinary day.

Most organisations do not need a louder AI strategy. They need a clearer operating model for where AI can reduce friction, improve coordination, and support better decisions.

Useful AI work usually starts with ordinary questions: where does information stall, where do teams repeat low-value work, where does reporting arrive too late, and where does knowledge live outside the systems people use every day.

The implementation path is often smaller and more disciplined than the hype suggests. Map the workflow, identify the decision points, improve the data surface, then introduce automation or intelligence where it genuinely changes the work.

AI becomes valuable when it is part of a system people can trust, operate, and improve.

That is the whole game. Not the model, not the demo, but the system around it that survives a normal Tuesday.
