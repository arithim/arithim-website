--- title
kicker: Keynote · AI governance
# Hiring the ==machine workforce==
notes: This talk sets out a lifecycle for AI agents borrowed from the way organisations already hire and manage staff, from requisition to retirement.

--- list
kicker: The hook
## Would you give a new hire production access on day one?
- No job description
- No manager
- No probation period
notes: Most people in the room would say no. Any one of those gaps would worry an HR team or an auditor.

--- statement
kicker: The gap
## We do it with AI agents ==all the time==.
Agents go live with none of the three. Then someone asks who is responsible for them.
notes: An agent often gets built quickly, connected to a few systems and switched on. The question of who answers for it usually comes later, after something has gone wrong.

--- list numbered
kicker: The idea
## Onboard agents the way you onboard people.
- Requisition | Someone asks for it and says why
- Job description | What it does, and what it must never do
- Named owner | One person who answers for it
- Supervised start | A person checks its work at first
- Earned autonomy | More freedom as it builds a record
- Retirement | A planned way to switch it off
notes: These are the six stages from requisition to retirement. One rule runs across all of them: a model upgrade counts as a re-hire.

--- cards
kicker: Step 1
## Write a ==job description==.
- What it does | The work it takes on and the systems it needs.
- What it must never do | The never list: actions too risky to hand over at any level of trust.
notes: The never list is usually the more useful half. An invoice agent might draft payment runs, while its never list says it must not approve a payment or change bank details.

--- statement
kicker: Step 2
## Name an owner. A ==person==, not a team.
notes: A team cannot be called into a meeting, and teams get reorganised. The owner answers for what the agent does, decides when it gets more freedom and decides when it stops.

--- quote
> Who owns the agent when the person who built it leaves?
cite: A question to ask in the room
notes: Often the builder became the owner by default and nobody planned for their departure. Ownership should pass to a new name before they go.

--- cards
kicker: Step 3
## Autonomy is ==earned==.
- Day one | Supervised. A person reviews its output or approves its actions.
- Over time | More freedom as it shows a record of doing the job well.
notes: This works like probation for a new hire. Record each agent's current level of autonomy and who granted it, so anyone can see it.

--- statement
kicker: Step 4
## A model upgrade is a ==re-hire==.
Same name, different employee. Re-check it.
notes: When the model behind an agent changes, the agent keeps its name and its access, but the reasoning comes from something new. Check it against its job description and never list again, and consider putting it back under supervision until it has a new record.

--- statement
kicker: Step 5
## Give it a ==retirement step==.
Old agents should not keep running quietly.
notes: When a process changes, the old agent should be switched off, its access removed and its record closed. Otherwise it keeps holding credentials nobody remembers granting.

--- statement
kicker: Why it matters
## This is the shape an ==auditor== wants to see.
If you cannot say who owns an agent and what it may do, you cannot evidence control. And you cannot govern agents you cannot list.
notes: The lifecycle produces the evidence as it goes. The requisition is the inventory entry, the job description and never list define what the agent may do, and the owner is the accountable person.

--- list
kicker: The checklist
## One page ==per agent==.
- Job description, with a never list
- Named human owner
- Current autonomy level
- Model upgrade triggers a re-check
- Retirement step
notes: Keep the page wherever your other system records live. If a page is hard to fill in, the agent is probably not under control yet.

--- list numbered
kicker: On Monday
## Start ==small==.
- List every agent you run
- Name an owner for each
- Write each one a never list
- Treat the next model upgrade as a re-hire
notes: None of this needs new software. HR already knows how to write a role and run a probation period, and technology knows what access an agent holds, so agree who runs each stage and write it down.
