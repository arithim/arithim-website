An enterprise AI assistant is the general-purpose tool employees open to write, research, analyse and hand work to agents, as opposed to an assistant built into one application such as an HR or contact centre system. Gartner's [Magic Quadrant for Enterprise AI Assistants](https://www.gartner.com/doc/reprints?id=00ThR00000DnlhSUAR&ct=260902&st=sb) is the first it has published for this market. It assessed ten vendors and estimates the market at $17 billion in 2025, rising to $71 billion by 2030. This piece follows our earlier article on [what agentic AI is and where it is working](insight-what-is-agentic-ai-where-is-it-working.html).

## What Gartner expects an assistant to do

To count as an enterprise AI assistant, a product needs an orchestrator that picks models, tools and data sources for each request, ready-made connectors to common business applications, and search over company data that respects existing permissions. It also needs an agent ecosystem with built-in, custom and third-party agents, and enterprise security covering identity integration, data loss prevention (DLP), monitoring of agent activity and standards such as GDPR and SOC 2. Central administration, an adoption dashboard and support for local languages, formats and data residency complete the list.

Gartner says the market is moving from chat and retrieval towards long-running, multistep work carried out across systems, and that this is now the main way vendors set themselves apart. It also notes that most organisations already run two or more assistants.

## Four questions that narrow the field

### Where does your work already happen?

Suite vendors bundle the assistant with their productivity platform, and Gartner says this shapes the experience. Microsoft Copilot draws on Microsoft 365 through Work IQ, but Gartner warns it needs a well-governed estate, including work on overshared SharePoint data, which can slow a rollout. Google's experience is strongest inside Workspace, and organisations on Microsoft 365 may find gaps such as missing native meeting summaries and office co-editing.

The independents, Anthropic, OpenAI and Glean, avoid that lock-in, but their reach into business systems varies. Gartner says OpenAI focuses on productivity tools and supports SAP, Workday and ServiceNow less well, that many of Glean's connectors to those systems are read-only, and that Anthropic has made broader connectors a roadmap priority. If your heaviest users live in an ERP or service management system, test the connectors they would actually use.

### Where does your data have to live?

Regional, sovereign and government hosting options vary widely, and Gartner says a vendor without the right regional footprint simply cannot be implemented by a non-U.S. or heavily regulated buyer. Microsoft has the broadest global and sovereign reach, and Google also covers regulated industries widely. OpenAI hosts mainly in North America and Europe. Anthropic hosts Claude Enterprise in the U.S. only; outside the U.S. its models are available through Amazon Bedrock, Microsoft Foundry and Google Cloud, without all of the assistant's features. Zoom is limited to U.S. data centres, Superhuman to North America, and ClickUp offers no government or sovereign cloud.

For a UK or EU organisation with residency obligations, that short list is worth settling before any feature comparison.

### How much agent governance do you need?

Gartner calls governance often decisive. Most assistants include some agent management, but managing agents from other providers usually costs extra. Google includes strong agent governance at no extra charge. Microsoft sells it through Agent 365, a separate per-user licence. Anthropic and OpenAI both rely on a compliance API connected to third-party security tools for long-term auditing and DLP, which Gartner flags as extra work for regulated buyers. Superhuman has no DLP or agent life cycle management.

Gartner's advice is to set up a cross-functional governance framework before a broad deployment, and to agree across workplace, security, compliance and identity teams who may create agents and on which platforms.

### Can you forecast the bill?

Pricing ranges from per-user licences to billing for every interaction, with metered agent usage on top of seats at several vendors. Anthropic is one of the few that bills all interactions, chat included, on consumption alongside a seat licence, which Gartner says makes budgeting harder. Microsoft combines per-user Copilot licences with usage-based Copilot Cowork, parts of Copilot Studio and the Agent 365 licence. Its E7 bundle simplifies some of that, but a Gartner survey in May 2026 found only 4% of IT leaders highly likely to adopt E7 within 12 months. Google adds each user licence to an organisation-wide consumption quota, with chat and search in the base price.

Gartner recommends plans that include AI usage in the seat price with some allowance for advanced agent actions, spending caps by department, and avoiding multiyear lock-in where possible.

## How we would run the choice

We would run the choice in this order, which follows Gartner's recommendations.

1. Settle hosting and residency first, because that removes vendors outright.
2. Map where your largest user groups spend their day, and which business systems they need the assistant to act in.
3. Pick two candidates and pilot each with the group that should gain most, as Gartner advises, instead of defaulting to the vendor you already pay.
4. Measure usage and outcomes through each product's adoption dashboard, and model the consumption bill from real pilot usage before signing.
5. Write down who may build agents, where, and who approves them, before rollout goes wider.

Gartner names low AI literacy, weak adoption, poor training and difficulty proving return as the most common problems when these programmes scale. Its answer is an adoption programme with an executive sponsor, AI champions and agreed success measures, with value shown early.

If you end up with two assistants, which Gartner thinks likely, the overlap is a cost to manage. Each brings its own agents, connectors, admin console and consumption meter, and the governance work grows with them. We covered the wider question of what organisations actually need from AI in [what businesses need from AI](insight-what-businesses-need-from-ai.html).

## Where agents help

The work that follows the purchase is mostly administrative and spans tools that do not talk to each other. We would build an oversight agent that reads the adoption dashboards, admin logs and billing data from whichever assistants a customer already runs, through their admin and compliance APIs with read-only access. Each week it would report usage by team, consumption spend against the caps finance has set, agents created and who created them, and seats that have gone unused.

When it finds something to change, such as an unused licence, a department heading past its cap or an agent built outside the agreed platforms, it drafts the change and a named person in IT or finance approves it before anything happens. That keeps the multivendor approach Gartner expects affordable, and gives the governance framework a record of what the assistants are actually doing.
