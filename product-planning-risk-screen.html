WHAT YOU GET

What the screen reports

Farmland

How much of the site falls in each agricultural land grade, and how much of that rests on a detailed survey.

Peat

How much deep peat there is and how far away it is.

Heritage

Listed buildings, monuments, parks and gardens, battlefields and World Heritage Sites on and around the site.

Protected sites

National Parks, designated landscapes and habitats, ancient woodland and wild land, with share and distance.

Other constraints

Flood zones, green belt, open access land, and distance to aerodromes and built-up areas.

GOOD TO KNOW

Good to know

  • The screen covers Great Britain.
  • Farmland grades come mostly from predictive and provisional maps. A soil survey confirms them on the ground.
  • Peat depth needs probing on site. The screen reports mapped peat only.
  • Glint and glare, conservation areas and archaeology records are not included.
  • The screen does not predict a planning decision.
FREQUENTLY ASKED

Questions about Planning risk screen

Does it tell me if my site is best and most versatile land?

It reports how much of the site is mapped as grades 1, 2 and 3a, and how much of that comes from a detailed survey. Where the map is predictive, it says so, and a soil survey is still needed.

Does it cover Scotland?

Yes. In Scotland it uses prime agricultural land, mapped peat classes, heritage data and designations.

Which listed buildings does it count?

Those on the site and in the area around it, with the highest grades counted separately, and the distance to the nearest of each.

Does it replace a consultant's survey?

No. It helps you decide whether surveys are worth paying for.

Does it predict planning outcomes?

No. It reports constraints and the studies you will probably need. The planning authority decides.
