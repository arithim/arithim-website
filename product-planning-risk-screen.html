PLANNING RISK SCREEN · GREAT BRITAIN

Check a site for planning constraints before you pay for surveys.

The planning risk screen is an Arithim desk screen of one site in Great Britain. It reports the constraints most likely to cause trouble for a solar, battery or wind scheme: farmland grade, peat, heritage and protected sites. It lists the field studies you will probably need before you apply.

WHAT YOU GET

What does the screen report?

Each constraint comes with its share of the site and its distance, so you can see which ones matter.

Farmland

The share of the site in each agricultural land classification grade, the share that comes from a detailed survey, and the share of prime land in Scotland.

Peat

The share of deep peat and its distance, for England and Wales, and classes 1 and 2 in Scotland.

Heritage

Listed buildings on and around the site, with Grade I and II* counted separately, plus scheduled monuments, registered parks and gardens, registered battlefields and World Heritage Sites, each with share and distance.

Protected sites

National Parks, National Landscapes and National Scenic Areas, SSSI, SAC, SPA, Ramsar, National Nature Reserves, ancient woodland and Wild Land Areas, each with share and distance.

Other constraints

Flood zones, green belt, open access and common land, and distance to aerodromes and built-up areas.

The study list

The field studies the application will probably need.

WHAT YOU SEND

What do I send?

  • A boundary, or a point with a size
  • The technology: solar, battery or wind

You receive a PDF and the same results as data. The layers come from open public data published by the national agriculture, nature, heritage and flood agencies, each credited.

WHO IT IS FOR

Who is the planning risk screen for?

Solar, battery and wind developers, their planning consultants and land agents. The question is whether a constraint on this parcel is likely to sink the application, before they pay for surveys.

LIMITS

What does the screen not do?

We state these on the page because they change what you can rely on.

  • Great Britain only.
  • Farmland grades come mostly from predictive and provisional maps. Field level grades need an auger survey by a soil scientist.
  • Peat depth on the site needs probing. The screen reports mapped peat classes only.
  • The engine works on 50 m pixels in cells of about 0.7 km², so narrow features and small buffers are indicative.
  • Aerodrome and Ministry of Defence safeguarding zones are not covered.
  • Glint and glare is not modelled. The screen lists it as a study to commission.
  • Conservation areas, local listings and archaeology records are not included.
  • It does not predict a planning decision.
FREQUENTLY ASKED

Questions about Planning risk screen

Does it tell me if my site is best and most versatile land?

It reports the share of the site mapped as grades 1, 2 and 3a, which is best and most versatile land, and how much of that comes from a detailed survey. Where the map is predictive, the screen says so, and a soil survey is still needed to confirm it.

Does it cover Scotland?

Yes. In Scotland it uses prime agricultural land, NatureScot peat classes 1 and 2, Historic Environment Scotland heritage data and NatureScot designations.

Which listed buildings does it count?

Listed buildings on the site and in the ring of cells around it, with Grade I and II* counted separately, and the distance to the nearest of each.

Does it replace a consultant's survey?

No. It is a desk screen to help you decide whether surveys are worth paying for.

Does it predict planning outcomes?

No. It reports constraints and the studies you will probably need. The planning authority decides.
