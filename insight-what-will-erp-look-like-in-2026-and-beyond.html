What Will ERP Look Like in 2026 and Beyond?

ERP · RESEARCH·24 NOV 2026·9 MIN READ·BY TARUN BULCHANDANI

ERP is moving from a system that records transactions to one where AI does much of the routine work and people supervise it. Gartner's Predicts 2026 report expects AI to execute more than half of routine ERP tasks by 2030, AI tools to cut modernisation costs by 40%, and a third of ERP selections to turn on the vendor's marketplace. It also expects more than 70% of the ERP AI capabilities companies buy to end up as shelfware, which makes adoption after go-live the part most plans leave out.

This article belongs with our pieces on where agentic AI is working in 2026, why AI pilots stall before production and process intelligence and process mining.

The short version

Gartner's Predicts 2026: The Future of ERP, published in December 2025, makes five predictions for 2030. More than half of routine ERP tasks will be executed autonomously by AI. A third of ERP selections will be decided by what the vendor's application marketplace offers. AI tools will cut modernisation costs by 40%. More than 70% of the ERP AI capabilities companies acquire will become shelfware. And geopolitics will push 75% of net-new ERP deployments for large enterprises in Europe and Asia/Pacific onto sovereign cloud.

Read together, the report describes ERP becoming a platform that does routine work and surfaces insight, with people overseeing it. It also says most organisations are not yet ready to use what vendors are building, because of weak data, little planning after go-live and change fatigue.

AI will run more of the routine work

The first prediction, analysed by Johan Jartelius, is that AI will execute more than 50% of routine ERP tasks by 2030, with less human involvement in finance, supply chain and HR. Gartner ties this to AI agents, which it defines as autonomous or semiautonomous software that perceives its environment, makes decisions, takes actions and pursues goals on its own. Its view is that agents move ERP away from manual, planning-heavy work towards a leaner experience that draws insight from company data.

Gartner names two obstacles. It expects that by 2027 only 30% of organisations will have data of good enough quality to use advanced AI capabilities. Heavily customised ERP estates will also take up new AI functions more slowly, so Gartner advises a more standard core with extensions around it.

Gartner also expects vendors to move AI towards usage-based pricing, which means a company needs to know which use cases it is paying for to avoid overruns. And as routine processing is automated, staff spend more time supervising agent output, which needs investment in AI literacy across the organisation. Gartner's recommendation is to start building an AI-ready data foundation now, covering quality, governance and integration.

Marketplaces will shape ERP selection

Tomas Kienast's prediction is that by 2030 a third of ERP selections will be decided by the business capabilities in the vendor's application marketplace, as well as by the core product.

The reasoning starts with multitenant cloud ERP. It pushes customers towards standard processes, which exposes gaps for companies that used to customise for industry depth. Vendors are responding by handing deep industry functions and localisations to partners and asking customers to source them through certified marketplaces. Gartner sees these marketplaces becoming a workable way to deliver last-mile functionality, and names AI agent marketplaces as an emerging point of difference between vendors.

This creates a governance problem. Capabilities that used to sit in the core product now come from third parties, so buyers have to judge how far the ERP vendor stands behind a partner's product on support, security and long-term viability. Gartner recommends deciding which capabilities must stay the ERP vendor's responsibility and which you will source from its ecosystem, then adding a formal partner assessment to your selection method that covers integration depth, accountability, support and pricing.

Modernisation will get cheaper

ERP upgrades are known for being slow and expensive. Gartner predicts AI tools will reduce modernisation costs by 40% by 2030. The report lists where the savings come from. AI helps migrate custom code, shortens regression testing, assists with data cleansing, discovers and maps integrations and keeps them current, and sets up configuration through agents.

Gartner expects vendors, service providers and internal IT teams to invest heavily in these tools, and IT roles to shift towards continuous improvement and risk management. Organisations that stay with labour-intensive integration will keep paying the old costs. Its recommended starting point is to pilot AI tools in low-risk areas such as automated testing, data cleansing or document generation, building internal skill and checking vendor claims before committing.

Most purchased ERP AI may go unused

Gartner expects more than 70% of acquired ERP AI capabilities to become shelfware by 2030, which bears directly on contracts companies are signing now.

The report gives three causes. After a resource-heavy migration, teams put stability first and keep postponing the AI tools they committed to at the start, because those tools need time and skills nobody allocated. Fear of missing out leads companies to buy AI add-ons or premium AI tiers without a specific business case, and those features stay switched off. Where adoption does begin, unexpected consumption costs and unplanned data preparation lead teams to stop using the features to protect the budget.

Gartner calls the gap between committed spend and actual use "AI value debt". It expects finance leaders to push back, asking vendors for pricing that covers the core product only, and expects a market for AI adoption services to grow as integrators help customers apply what they have bought. Vendor competition should shift from shipping features quickly to proving customer adoption.

The advice is to make AI buying governance-led. Require a clear business case and predictable return before acquiring a capability. Negotiate the right to test in non-production environments without consumption fees. Then treat adoption after go-live as a formal phase with owners and resources, so it does not become an optional follow-on that never happens. Our article on why AI pilots stall before production covers the same pattern outside ERP.

Sovereignty will change where ERP runs

The fifth prediction, analysed by Neha Ralhan, is that geopolitical factors will lead 75% of net-new ERP deployments for large enterprises in Europe and Asia/Pacific to choose sovereign cloud by 2030.

Gartner reports that rising geopolitical tension and regulation are moving organisations towards local and regional cloud providers, particularly in Europe, Asia/Pacific and Canada. Sovereignty requirements differ by country, industry and workload, and they come from business needs as well as law. ERP vendors are building their own sovereign offerings, hyperscalers are launching in-region clouds kept apart from their global regions, and some domestic companies are building national clouds on hyperscale technology. Interest in staying on premises has also risen, because it keeps data under direct control.

Sovereign cloud versions generally receive new features more slowly, so a company may have to accept slower access to AI functions in exchange for compliance. Gartner recommends working with legal, compliance and risk teams to define your requirements by country, industry and workload, and then testing how each vendor meets them, bearing in mind that many vendors define sovereignty in their own terms.

What this means for an ERP plan in 2026

Taken together, the predictions suggest a few practical steps for anyone selecting, upgrading or running ERP over the next few years.

Work on data before features. Gartner's figure that only 30% of organisations will have AI-ready data by 2027 is the constraint behind most of the other predictions. Autonomous tasks, AI-assisted modernisation and adoption after go-live all depend on clean, governed and integrated data.

Buy AI capabilities against named use cases. The shelfware prediction is a warning about contracts signed on a vendor's roadmap. Each AI add-on should have an owner, a measure of success and a plan for when it will be switched on.

Widen the selection scorecard. Marketplace partners, sovereignty options and AI pricing models now belong in an ERP evaluation alongside core functionality.

Keep the core standard. Customisation slows the adoption of new AI functions, and Gartner's view is that marketplace partners and extensions are the better route to industry depth.

The report's look back is a useful check on how far to trust timelines. Gartner says its earlier prediction that the top four ERP vendors would reposition as business platforms by 2025 was effectively met. Its prediction that citizen developers would make more than 65% of post-implementation ERP changes by 2025 was not, because low-code applications are still mostly built by IT professionals.

Where agents help

The shelfware prediction describes a problem agents are well placed to address, because the capability is already paid for and the missing piece is the work of putting it to use.

The agent we would build sits beside the ERP a customer already runs. It reads the licence and contract records to list which AI features have been bought, checks usage logs to see which are switched on and used, and drafts a short case for each idle feature, naming the process it would help, the data it needs and what that data looks like today. The process owner decides which ones to pursue. For those, the agent prepares the data quality fixes and the configuration changes, and a person approves each one before it is applied. Once a feature is live, the agent tracks usage and consumption costs against the agreed measure and reports back when either drifts.

The same pattern applies to modernisation work Gartner calls low risk, such as regression testing and data cleansing, where an agent can do the repetitive part and leave the decisions with the team. Our article on where agentic AI is working in 2026 sets out the wider picture.

Common questions

What are the biggest ERP trends for 2026?

Gartner's Predicts 2026 report names five. AI agents taking over routine ERP tasks, vendor marketplaces becoming a deciding factor in selection, AI tools lowering the cost of ERP modernisation, purchased AI features going unused, and geopolitics pushing large enterprises towards sovereign cloud.

Will AI replace people in ERP?

Gartner expects AI to execute more than 50% of routine ERP tasks by 2030, which reduces human involvement in finance, supply chain and HR. It describes people moving from processing transactions to supervising what AI agents produce, and says organisations need to invest in AI literacy so staff can check and trust that output.

Why do ERP AI features go unused?

Gartner predicts that more than 70% of acquired ERP AI capabilities will become shelfware by 2030. The causes it gives are teams postponing AI work after a draining migration, AI add-ons bought without a business case, unpredictable consumption costs and data preparation that nobody planned for. Its advice is to require a business case before buying and to run adoption as a formal, resourced phase after go-live.

What is sovereign cloud ERP?

It is ERP run on cloud infrastructure that meets a country's or region's rules on data location, control and access. Gartner expects geopolitical factors to drive 75% of net-new ERP deployments for large enterprises in Europe and Asia/Pacific to sovereign cloud by 2030, and warns that sovereign offerings usually receive new features more slowly.

How much can AI reduce the cost of an ERP upgrade?

Gartner predicts AI tools will cut ERP modernisation costs by 40% by 2030, through help with code migration, testing, data cleansing, integration mapping and configuration. It recommends starting with low-risk work such as automated testing, data cleansing or document generation to build skills and check vendor claims.
