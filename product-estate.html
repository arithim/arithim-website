THE SMALLER FEATURES

How the smaller features work

Thirty-two smaller features sit around the ten above, grouped by the loop. Each says what it does and how it behaves when it runs.

Discover

Early access

Shadow app and risk triage

On the first sighting of an unapproved app, the product scores its risk, checks for overlap and asks the first user what it is for and what data goes in. A free, low-risk app with five users or fewer and no overlap is marked Allowed. Overlap triggers an offer of the approved app. Blocking is advisory, and revoking access is always a ticket. This is the same discovery that catches vibe-coded apps built outside IT.

Early access

Spend matching and contract capture

Card descriptors and invoices are cleaned and matched to vendors, invoices are split by line, and currency is converted at the transaction-date rate. Contract terms are extracted by AI and stay Inferred until a named person confirms them. The product never reads full card numbers and never makes payments.

Early access

Ownership and freshness chase

Each application has a business, technical and budget owner. The product suggests an owner with the reason, such as "212 active users in Marketing, renews 14 March", and asks Accept, Not me or Ask a question. Leavers' apps move to their manager the same day, and nobody gets more than one question or nudge per app a week.

Soon

Technology risk and standards

For custom, hosted and desktop software, the product joins versions to end-of-support dates and to your adopt, trial, contain and retire standards. The technical owner picks Plan upgrade, Accept risk until a date or Already handled. Accepted risk expires and re-triggers.

Soon

Observed interfaces

App-to-app OAuth grants, API connections and MCP links seen by the feeds are recorded as interfaces, so the impact of retiring an application shows what depends on it.

Decide

Early access

Seat pool, access and purchase

Access requests pick a tier, role and duration and follow the policy's approval chain. A pooled seat is used first, and the seat expires by ticket. Use is checked after 30 days. Purchases go in parallel to the budget owner, security, the architect and procurement, and the signed contract creates the vendor, application and renewal. Privileged roles never self-serve.

Soon

Demand triage

A daily digest proposes a route for each demand: fast track, one-screen case, full case, merge or reject with a reason. The demand manager accepts or changes it in one click. Findings and renewals never create initiatives on their own.

Soon

Business case

The product drafts options, cost lines and benefit lines, each bound to a measure such as "matched spend reaches zero". The project manager writes up to 300 words and a sponsor confirms. Above the board threshold, finance reviews and the portfolio board approves.

Soon

Prioritisation and scoring

Initiatives are scored for strategic fit, financial return, cost removed, risk reduction and delivery confidence, ranked, and a funding line is drawn. Manual moves need a visible reason, and the board approves the funded list. Estimated effort is totalled by team per quarter.

Soon

Rationalisation and retirement proposals

Candidates are ranked by saving multiplied by confidence, with a target app. The impact view shows users, interfaces, data to export and the last safe notice day. On approval it raises a demand carrying the evidence, fast-tracked if small.

Stage gates and change control

Each gate pack marks every item evidenced, attested or missing. At the build gate the product checks that the review passed with no open conditions, that security and data protection are signed off and that a baseline exists. Derived status

Schedule, cost, scope, adoption, risk and overdue conditions each get a rating from the data. The project manager gets a card to confirm, edit or override with a reason. Soon

Roadmap, risks and conflicts

The roadmap is derived from each initiative's impacts and milestones. The product detects conflicts such as a retirement after a notice date, a capability left with no Invest app, or two initiatives on one app in a quarter, and posts each to the risk log as a suggestion with evidence.

Early access

Offboarding

For each leaver the product computes their footprint, checks identity deactivation, raises one ticket per non-SSO account, returns licences to the pool and proposes ownership transfer to the manager. It also flags agents, keys and card subscriptions under the leaver's identity. It deactivates and never deletes, and a legal hold stops destructive steps.

Early access

Access reviews

Leaver revocations are accepted automatically, privileged access needs a second reviewer, and there is no approve-all on it. The campaign closes with an evidence pack for auditors.

AI IN THE PRODUCT

Where AI helps, and who decides

AI drafts, suggests and summarises inside each flow. It acts alone only on a reversible change to the product's own records, above a confidence threshold. A named person decides everything else, and every AI suggestion is labelled as one.

Matching names and spend

Decides only when it is very sure, and suggests otherwise. Every merge can be split.

Reading contracts

Drafts the dates, notice period, value and terms. A named person confirms them before any clock uses them.

Mapping capabilities and TIME

Suggests links and positions, marked Inferred. Owners and the architect confirm the ones that matter.

Review packs, business cases and status

Drafts options, cases and up to 120 words of weekly status. The architect, the project manager or the sponsor decides.

Access review recommendations

Suggests keep or revoke with a reason, and accepts leaver revocations. A reviewer decides the rest.

The MCP server and packs

Answers questions over your data and drafts the narrative of a pack. A person approves any proposal and releases any pack.

AI never sends anything to a vendor, never approves a write to one of your systems, never reads prompt content and never changes a Confirmed value. If your people need help owning these decisions, see our AI readiness assessment and AI training.

SECURITY AND CONTROL

Security, data and control

We give your security team a scope manifest for each connector on the first call: every scope, the fields it yields, why we need it, how long we keep it and which feature uses it. For regulated teams we also build compliance workflows with an audit trail.

Two consents, not one

The first covers read-only access to your systems of record, plus messages, tickets and, if you choose, epics. The second follows your first report and covers the browser extension, device management, AI vendor reports and opt-in group removal after 30 days in review-only mode.

What we never ask for

Mailbox or calendar content, chat history, salary or protected characteristics, prompts or outputs from AI tools, full card numbers, write access to finance, ERP or HR systems, passwords, or network scanners on your estate.

Guardrails on every write

30 days review-only for a new rule, allow-listed apps and groups, deactivate and never delete, capped batches, exemptions, one-click undo for 14 days, a kill switch per rule and an exportable log of who approved what.

Your region, your retention

Tenant data stays in its region: the EU and the US at launch, with the UK and Australia on request. Raw usage events are kept for 90 days and daily roll-ups for 25 months.

Leaving is designed in

Continuous export of the full dataset to a bucket you own, a documented schema, a read API and a contractual wind-down of at least 90 days.

Security paperwork

A completed CAIQ and SIG Lite, a data processing agreement with standard contractual clauses, a sub-processor list and a penetration test summary. SOC 2 Type I is planned for launch, with Type II and ISO 27001 within twelve months.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

Questions about Arithim Estate

What is Arithim Estate?

A system of record for the technology a company runs, what it pays for, why it has it and what will change it. It combines SaaS management, enterprise architecture and portfolio management on one set of records, so the three views agree.

How do I find shadow AI in my company?

Connect read-only to your identity provider, finance and card feeds and the AI vendors' admin reports. Arithim Estate finds AI tools, assistants and agents that nobody registered, shows who uses them, what they cost and what data they can reach, and asks the owner to confirm. It records metadata only and never reads prompts or outputs.

Does it help with AI governance and the EU AI Act?

It gives you the inventory and the owner, risk tier and review records those programmes start from. It is not legal advice. If you also need dated proof of which AI models you depend on, pair it with the AI model inventory.

Is it SaaS management, enterprise architecture or PMO software?

All three, on the same data. The SaaS management core is always included. The architecture and portfolio tiers add capability mapping, reviews and decisions on one side and demand, cases, gates and benefits on the other.

How long until I see value?

The first report arrives within ten working days of connecting read-only to your identity provider, finance export, card feed and contracts. It shows spend, unused seats, shadow IT and shadow AI, and renewals by notice date.

Does it change anything in our systems?

In the first release it writes messages in Slack, Teams and email, tickets in one queue you name and, if you switch it on, epics in one delivery project. With your second consent it can also remove people from allow-listed licence groups after 30 days in review-only mode. It never writes to finance, ERP or HR systems.

Can we start with only the SaaS management core?

Yes. You still get the front door, the seat pool, the renewal clock, licence reclamation and the savings ledger. Requests that need a bigger decision wait with a named approver until you add a tier.

How does it find AI tools and agents we did not register?
